# What to say instead of threats: swap "if you don't…" for words that actually work

"If you don't put your shoes on, there's no park!" It worked last month. Now your child shrugs, and you hear yourself saying the same sentence again, only louder.

Instead of "If you don't…, then…", say the same limit as a plan: "When your shoes are on, we'll go to the park." Offer a choice you're fine with ("By yourself, or should I help?"), or say what you'll do ("I'm turning the screen off now"). If you need a consequence, make it small, linked to the act, said once, and something you will actually do. Below: four swaps, a table you can screenshot, what to do if you already threatened, and the threats to drop for good.

## Why threats stop working

A threat puts the focus on the punishment, not on what to do now. The [CDC's "Tips for Giving Directions"](https://www.cdc.gov/parenting-toddlers/directions/good-directions.html) (30 April 2026) says to tell the child what to do, not just what to stop.

Threats also escalate: small, then bigger, then impossible ("no birthday!"). When a threat is empty and not carried out, children learn that words don't mean much. [Caring for Kids](https://caringforkids.cps.ca/handouts/behavior-and-development/positive-discipline-for-young-children) (updated January 2020) puts it simply: say what you mean, mean what you say, and follow through.

Harsh words and shaming are ineffective and harmful, according to [HealthyChildren (AAP)](https://www.healthychildren.org/english/family-life/family-dynamics/communication-discipline/pages/disciplining-your-child.aspx) (updated 5 November 2018). No research claims beyond that, and no promise that a different sentence will "stop" the moment.

## Four swaps

The same limit, said as a plan:

1. **When / then:** "When your shoes are on, we'll go to the park."
2. **A real choice** (only if both options are OK for you): "Shoes by yourself, or should I help?"
3. **Here's what I'm going to do:** "I'm turning the screen off now." Or: "I'm going to help your feet walk to the car." Israel's Ministry of Health parenting page [on stepping out of the anger cycle](https://me.health.gov.il/parenting/learn-more/family-parental-welfare/anger-management/) (15 January 2025, Hebrew) says to say what you will do to help the child keep the limit, and what *is* allowed.
4. **One small consequence,** said once, linked to the act, that you'll actually carry out: "If the blocks get thrown, the blocks go up for today." The CDC suggests asking yourself first if you have the time and energy to follow through.

### Threat or consequence?

A threat is big, far away, angry, and often not carried out. A consequence is small, linked, immediate, said once in a normal voice, and done.

### When they still don't move

Act, calmly: help the shoes on, carry, turn off. No new threat, and no lecture. Repeating threats teaches that the first words don't count.

## Already threatened?

If something too big slipped out, say so simply and replace it:

> "I said no birthday party. That was too much, and I'm not doing that. Here's what is happening: the blocks go up for today."

Then follow through on the small one.

## Threats to drop for good

Some sentences work through fear, not understanding. Better to drop them entirely. No clinical claims here, just a clear direction:

- Leaving the child behind ("Bye, I'm going without you").
- Scary authority figures ("The police or the guard will take you").
- Threats about love, or about giving the child away.
- Threats that use food or sleep as punishment.

Instead of "I'm leaving without you": "Time to go. I'll hold your hand to the car." Then walk them there.

## Instead of this, say this

| Instead of this | Say this | Why it works |
| --- | --- | --- |
| "If you don't put your shoes on, no park!" | "When your shoes are on, we'll go to the park." | Same limit, said as a plan instead of a punishment. |
| "Turn it off or I'm throwing the tablet away!" | "Screen goes off in two minutes. You press off, or I will." | A warning, a choice, then you act. No impossible promise. |
| "I'm counting to three… one… two…" | "It's time. I'm going to help you now." | Action instead of a countdown that teaches waiting for "three". |
| "Bye, I'm leaving without you!" | "Time to go. I'll hold your hand to the car." | No fear of being left; you stay the safe adult. |
| "Hit him again and no birthday party!" | "I won't let you hit. I'm moving you over here." | Stops the act now instead of a punishment days away. |
| "Do you want a punishment?" | "Toys go in the box. Blocks first or cars first?" | A choice inside the limit instead of a dare. |
| "Stop screaming or we're turning around!" (and you won't) | "I can't drive safely with screaming. I'm pulling over until it's quieter." | A real, doable consequence instead of an empty one. |
| "If you don't eat, no dessert ever again!" | "Dinner's on the table till we clear. Next food is the evening snack." | Calm structure, no food-as-punishment. |

## When it's more than a hard moment

<aside class="blog-note" role="note">

Threats slip out of every parent. It's worth getting support if:

- threats, yelling or fear are part of most days and you can't stop, or you're afraid of losing control (Caring for Kids: talk to someone before it affects the relationship);
- you've hit or come close to hitting, or you feel mostly negative about your relationship with your child;
- your child seems frightened of you, or of being left.

</aside>

Where to turn:

- **Your child's pediatrician** (in Israel, through your HMO). Outside Israel: [find a pediatrician on HealthyChildren (AAP)](https://www.healthychildren.org/English/tips-tools/find-pediatrician/Pages/Pediatrician-Referral-Service.aspx).
- **[Tipat Halav (Family Health Centers, Israel's Ministry of Health)](https://me.health.gov.il/en/parenting/raising-children/infant-and-child-development/tipat-halav/)** — development and parenting questions up to age 6.
- **[Parent coaching](/en/blog/parent-coaching)** — which kind of help fits you.
- If you, the parent, are overwhelmed: **ERAN (Israel)**, emotional first aid by phone and online, anonymous. **1201**: [eran.org.il](https://www.eran.org.il/) · Parents' line **\*9061**: [ERAN for parents](https://www.eran.org.il/eran-for-the-community/parents/).
- In an emergency, call your local emergency services.
