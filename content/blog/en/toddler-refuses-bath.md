# Toddler refuses the bath (or the hair wash): what to say to get through bath time

"Bath time!" and your child is already under the table. Or they get in happily and scream the second water touches their hair.

When a toddler refuses the bath, break it into three small steps: getting in, the hair wash and getting out. Warn five minutes ahead and offer a small choice ("boat or duck?"). For the hair, cover their eyes, rinse with a cup and count three pours out loud. To get out, give a warning and a job. Some nights a quick wash with a cloth is enough. Never leave a young child alone in the bath. Below: one sentence for getting in, one for the hair wash, one for getting out, and a short table of swaps.

## Why bath time turns into a fight

Many toddlers go through a stage of fearing something about the bath: the drain noise, slipping, water or soap in their eyes, or the hair wash itself. That's what [Raising Children Network, "Fear of the bath"](https://raisingchildren.net.au/newborns/behaviour/common-concerns/fear-of-the-bath) describes. Bath time is also a transition away from play, at the most tired hour of the day.

Most refusal is ordinary resistance. A small group of children have real sensory difficulty — as the [occupational therapy service at Hillel Yaffe Medical Center](https://hymc.org.il/?ArticleID=10025&CategoryID=2447) (29 June 2023) sets out. When that's worth checking is in the box at the end of this page.

If the bath fight starts when the screen goes off, there is a separate guide: [screen-time meltdowns](/en/blog/screen-time-meltdown).

## Getting in

1. **Warn first:** "Bath in five minutes, after this tower." A bit of warning makes the transition easier, as Raising Children also notes.
2. **Choice, not whether:** "Boat or duck in the bath with you?" or "Bath or shower tonight?" Both options should be fine with you.
3. **Play the transition:** "Can you hop to the bath like a frog?" Israel's Ministry of Health parenting page, ["leaving the anger cycle"](https://me.health.gov.il/parenting/learn-more/family-parental-welfare/anger-management/) (15 January 2025), lists a defined choice and play as ways out of a stand-off.
4. **Say what you'll do, calmly:** "It's bath time. I'll carry you, or you can walk."

### Do you have to win tonight?

There's no need to force a very upset child — Raising Children says this too. A quick wash with a cloth tonight and hair tomorrow is a fine plan. Consistency over the week matters more than winning one night.

## The hair wash

- Cover the eyes: a dry cloth or goggles the child holds; lean the head forward, not back. That's the advice in the [NHS Children and Family Health Devon hairwashing leaflet](https://childrenandfamilyhealthdevon.nhs.uk/resources/hairwashing-for-children-tips-and-advice/).
- Rinse with a cup or your hand, at a low stream, not the shower head (NHS Devon; also Hillel Yaffe).
- Count out loud: "Three pours. One… two… three. Done."
- Let them do a part: they hold the cup, or lather their own hair.
- Same order every time, and keep it short.

## Getting out

A short warning: "Two more pours on the duck, then towel." A small job helps: "You pull the plug after you're out." Some children are afraid of the drain noise, so they're out first, and only then you pull the plug — as Raising Children suggests.

You can also turn the towel into a game: "Burrito time." If they still won't get out: "Bath is finished. I'm lifting you out now." Calm, once, with no threat about tomorrow.

Never leave a young child alone in the bath, even for a moment — [HealthyChildren, "Bathroom Safety"](https://www.healthychildren.org/English/safety-prevention/at-home/Pages/Bathroom-Safety.aspx) (updated 24 January 2017), and Israel's [Ministry of Health page on child water safety](https://me.health.gov.il/parenting/raising-children/safe-environment/home-safety/child-water-safety-drowning-prevention/) (23 April 2026).

## If the bath fight is really a bedtime fight

If the fight is about everything after the bath — more water, one more story — that's bedtime stalling, a different moment. This page stops when the child is dry.

## Instead of this, say this

| Instead of this | Say this | Why it works |
| --- | --- | --- |
| "Get in the bath NOW." | "Bath in five minutes. Boat or duck tonight?" | A warning and a choice give a small sense of control. |
| "Stop crying, it's just water." | "Water on your face feels awful to you. Hold this cloth over your eyes." | Names the feeling and gives them a tool. |
| "If you don't get in, no story." | "First bath, then story. I'm starting the water." | When/then instead of a threat; you act, they follow. |
| "Hold still!" (during the rinse) | "Three pours and we're done. One… two… three." | Counting makes the end visible. |
| "You're too big to be scared of the bath." | "The drain is loud. You can get out before I pull the plug." | Takes a real fear seriously and solves it. |
| "Fine, no bath ever again!" | "Tonight a quick wash with a cloth. Hair tomorrow." | A smaller plan beats a lost fight. |
| "Out NOW or no TV tomorrow!" | "Bath's finished. Burrito towel. I'm lifting you out." | Calm action, once, without a far-off threat. |

"If you don't get in, no story" is a classic threat. For swapping threat sentences in general, there is a separate guide: [instead of threats](/en/blog/instead-of-threats).

## When it's more than a hard moment

<aside class="blog-note" role="note">

Most bath refusal is ordinary. It's worth checking with your child's pediatrician or an occupational therapist if:

- the distress is extreme and keeps happening even when the bath is calm and gentle, **and** your child also reacts strongly to other sensations (food textures, noise, sand or paint on hands, certain clothes) (Hillel Yaffe Medical Center, OT service);
- a new, sudden fear of water appears after a scare (a slip, water up the nose) and doesn't ease over a few weeks;
- your child says the bath or a part of the body hurts, or you notice pain, redness or skin trouble. Check with the pediatrician.

</aside>

- **Your child's pediatrician** (in Israel, through your HMO). Outside Israel: [find a pediatrician on HealthyChildren (AAP)](https://www.healthychildren.org/English/tips-tools/find-pediatrician/Pages/Pediatrician-Referral-Service.aspx).
