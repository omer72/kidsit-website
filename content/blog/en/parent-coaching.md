# Parent coaching: what it is, when to reach out, and which help fits you

Parent coaching is a focused process with a professional that gives parents tools for a specific challenge with their child. It is not therapy for the child. On this page: when it is worth it, which options exist in Israel (some at no cost, and some paid), and what to do in the meantime, before there is an appointment.

> Kidsit is an AI app for the hard moment, not a parent coach and not therapy. It does not replace a professional, and you can use it alongside coaching.

## What is parent coaching?

The focus is the parent, not a diagnosis of the child. You arrive with something that keeps coming back: sleep, a limit, a tantrum, a fight between siblings, the morning at the daycare door. You leave with a way of acting that fits this home. You can come alone, with a partner, in a clinic or on Zoom, for private meetings or a group.

According to [Midrag](https://www.midrag.co.il/Content/Tip/21078), parent coaching focuses on parenting, on the relationship with the child, and on shaping a parenting approach. It can stand on its own, for the parents only, and it can also be added to individual therapy for the child. That page describes a process of between two and three months. That is their wording, not a promise that every coach works this way.

### What is the difference between parent coaching and therapy?

| | Who it focuses on | What you get | When this is the place |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Parent coaching | The parents | Tools and a way to act on a specific challenge | When you want to change what is happening at home |
| Emotional or psychological therapy | The child, the parent, or the family | In-depth work, and a diagnosis when one is needed | When there is distress, strong fear, or a difficulty that is not moving |
| Medical consultation | The child | A medical check | With a pediatrician |

Midrag distinguishes coaching from couples therapy or psychological therapy: therapy, in their wording, focuses on you, and coaching focuses on the parent you are. In an article by Dr. Moran Influs Adler on [Walla Health](https://www.walla.co.il/healthy/health-news/3629618) (updated January 7, 2024), it says that when a child has significant symptoms that are hard to treat through the parents, individual therapy for the child may need to be added. When there is a concern about development, health, or safety, coaching is not enough, and neither is an app.

### Who provides parent coaching?

Training varies. According to Midrag, parent coaching does not require a license or a formal certificate. Each coach follows a different path, with a different specialty and approach, so you check training, experience, and fit for the age.

The Walla Health article stresses checking the training, the knowledge, and whether there is experience with the difficulty you came with. It also says that clinical, developmental, and educational psychologists, and clinical social workers who specialize in work with children, studied for years and hold a professional license supervised by the Ministry of Health. A license like that is not a condition for offering coaching, and it does mark a supervised profession. The chemistry in the first conversation matters no less than the certificate.

## When is it worth reaching out for parent coaching?

There is no cutoff here of "after this many weeks." Parents reach out when something at home stays stuck, even if nobody has called it a problem.

Reasons Midrag lists as common: potty training, sleep, difficulty with limits, behavior, emotional or social difficulties (including anxiety, depression, and tantrums, in their wording), and changes such as a growing family, divorce, a move, death, or illness. The Walla article also adds disagreements between the parents about how to raise a child, children's fears, developmental delays, and difficulty in the relationship with the child.

In the language of an ordinary evening, it looks like this:

- The same struggle comes back almost every day: sleep, the morning, a screen, hitting.
- The house feels on edge, and there is no quiet moment to repair.
- The two parents do not agree on the approach, and the child hears two messages.
- A big change arrives: a brother or a sister, a move, a separation.

If you yelled and you are looking for a repair sentence, there is a separate guide: [I yelled at my child](/he/blog/tzakti-al-hayeled). A repair sentence is not a substitute for a process, if the yelling has become the routine.

This page does not diagnose. Diagnosed difficulties, such as attention deficit, are a reason Midrag mentions for reaching out, and the diagnosis itself stays with a professional.

## What kinds of help are there in Israel?

No option fits everyone. This is a map, not a ranking.

| Kind | Fits when… | What it looks like | Cost |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| A private parent coach | You want a personal process | Regular meetings, in a clinic or on Zoom | Paid, and it varies |
| A workshop or a parents' group | You want shared tools and other parents | Meetings by topic or by age | It varies |
| A public service | You want to start through a health fund, the Ministry of Health, or a municipality | A group meeting, a series of meetings, or a phone line | Some at no cost, some paid. Check eligibility |
| A book or a recorded course | You want to understand at your own pace, in the evening | Reading or watching | It varies |

### A private parent coach

According to Midrag, a private meeting is you and the coach, or you, the child, and the coach. They describe one meeting a week, 50 minutes, in a clinic or on Zoom, and the number of meetings is flexible: some people need a long process and some are looking for a single meeting. Zoom is one format within the coaching, not a separate kind of product.

The cost varies from coach to coach. Midrag publishes averages and price lists on its page, as of a reading of the page on November 2, 2026. Ask the price in the introductory conversation, including whether there is a package, and what happens between the meetings.

### A workshop or a parents' group

According to Midrag, a workshop is content set in advance, by age or by need, with room to share and to hear other parents. They describe one meeting a week, of about two hours, in person or online, and usually between 4 and 10 meetings. It fits when you want a shared language, and less so when you need someone who goes into the depth of your home.

### Public services

**Tiponet, Ministry of Health.** On the [Tiponet](https://me.health.gov.il/parenting/raising-children/infant-and-child-development/tipat-halav/tiponet/) page (March 31, 2025), Tiponet is described as an online platform for group meetings of parents, on a range of topics, led by Tipat Halav nurses. The meetings are at no cost. They are given in Hebrew and in Arabic. After registration, a link to the Zoom meeting is sent by email, and on the morning of the meeting also by text message. Topics on the page include caring for babies, sleep, nutrition, development, the relationship between siblings, and parents' well-being. You register through the page.

**Parent coaching at Maccabi, in the upapp app.** On the [parent coaching in upapp](https://www.maccabi4u.co.il/maccabi_circles/upapp/parent_trainings/) page (updated July 8, 2026) it says: up to 8 meetings per series, including an introductory meeting or several introductory meetings, with a team of specialists, at no cost. The service is given by a team of therapists that includes educational psychologists and licensed therapists. It is for parents dealing with challenges in the relationship with their children, and the page lists, among other things, parental burnout, difficulty with routine, limits, life changes, divorce, loss, gender identity, and sibling relationships. You register through mind in the upapp app. The service is available to all Maccabi members. People who are not Maccabi members are not covered by that sentence.

**Keshet Early Childhood Center, Tel Aviv-Yafo Municipality.** On the [Keshet Center](https://www.tel-aviv.gov.il/Residents/HealthAndSocial/Pages/keshet.aspx) page (checked on November 2, 2026), the center provides responses for young children and their parents, with an emphasis on the relationship between the parent and the child. The intervention can be individual, including parent coaching or counseling, or in a group. Under coaching and counseling for parents it says: coaching that includes 15 meetings, and/or short-term counseling of a few meetings, according to the difficulty and the family's needs. The service is given by a social worker or an early-childhood educational counselor. The page lists a price: 35 NIS for a one-time meeting, and 350 NIS for the full coaching. This is not coaching at no cost. On the same page, a postpartum mothers' group (eight meetings, with the babies, in Kiryat Shalom and Neve Ofer, led by social workers) is described as participation at no cost. Other services on the same page, such as dyadic therapy, are listed separately and with their own price.

**Kol Sherut.** On the [Kol Sherut for parents](https://www.kolsherut.org.il/parents) site there is a directory of services for parents, including a parent-coaching category, and you can see where each service operates. That does not mean every service there is at no cost. Read the service's page before you reach out.

Other health funds are not included here. Within this check, no official page of theirs was found that describes parent coaching in the same detail.

### Books and recorded courses

A book or a recorded course fits a quiet evening, when there is energy to read or to listen. They give language and principles. They are not in the living room at six, when the cup is the wrong color. The cost varies, and there is no recommendation here of a specific book.

## How do you choose a parent coach?

The introductory conversation is the place to ask, not to close. The list fits a coach of any gender.

- Is there a specialty in the child's age?
- What is the training, where did they study, and how much experience is there with this difficulty?
- Which approach do they work in?
- What is the goal of the process, and roughly how many meetings?
- In person or on Zoom?
- What happens between meetings: can you write, or do you wait until next week? [What to note between sessions and what to bring](/en/blog/prepare-for-parent-coaching-session).
- What is the cost, and what is included in it?

According to Midrag, because there is no license requirement, these questions are the check. If after the conversation it is not clear what will happen in the next meeting, that is an answer in itself.

## When not to rely on any of these alone

Coaching, a workshop, a book, and an app do not replace a check when there is a concern.

- A concern for the safety of the child or of someone in the home.
- A concern about development or health: talk to a pediatrician, and you can also go to Tipat Halav.
- The parent themselves is in distress that affects functioning.

The Ministry of Health publishes a page of [helplines for mental-health first aid](https://me.health.gov.il/mental-health/therapy-rehabilitation/crisis-support/first-aid/) (October 21, 2026). The page lists phone lines that operate around the clock, including ERAN at 1201, and also the mental-health support lines of the health funds. In extreme distress you can go to an emergency room at a mental-health center: according to the page, these emergency rooms operate around the clock, all year, no referral is required, and the visit does not involve a payment. If the distress comes with physical signs such as pressure in the chest, difficulty breathing, or fainting, that page says to seek urgent medical care or an emergency room according to the severity.

Do not copy a phone number from here to somewhere else without opening the page. The full list is on it.
